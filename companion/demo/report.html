6Method

How VST measures speed

A fixed camera looks across the street. VideoSpeedTracker (VST) finds moving vehicles by comparing consecutive frames, follows each one's front bumper, and counts the frames it takes to travel between two vertical lines drawn on the picture. Speed is the calibration speed scaled by the ratio of frame counts: speed = calibration speed × calibration frames ÷ frames counted, corrected for the video's frame rate. It measures time over a fixed distance; it does not use radio or laser.

Calibration

The distance between the lines is never measured directly. Instead a car drives through at a known speed (on cruise control) and VST records how many frames it took, separately for each direction because the near and far lanes are at different distances from the camera. A later check pass at a known speed shows how far off the calibration reads. A direction counts as verified when its latest check after its latest calibration is within 2 mph.

What is excluded and why

  • Vehicles timed under 18 mph (the logging minimum): mostly turning, parking or stopping, not free-flowing traffic.
  • Vehicles that couldn't be timed cleanly (two vehicles overlapping, a vehicle hidden behind another): their crossing can't be measured.
  • Speeds flagged as implausible (more than 20 mph over the “way over” setting): usually two vehicles merged into one; they're left out of every statistic.
  • Vehicles tracked across a camera dropout: dropped, so no speed is ever measured across a gap.

Statistics

Percentiles use the nearest-rank method: sort the n speeds; the p-th percentile is the value at position ⌈p/100 × n⌉. The 85th percentile is the speed at or below which 85% of vehicles travel, the figure traffic engineers use to review a speed limit. “Over the limit” means strictly above 25 mph. Hour-of-day figures use the local clock time of each vehicle, and hourly percentiles are shown only for hours with at least 5 vehicles. Coverage is the time between each run's start and end minus outages (from the run manifest); without a manifest it is the time from the first to the last vehicle.

Privacy

VST stores a speed, a direction and a time per vehicle. It does not read plates or recognise faces; the location is given only as a street and block.

Limitations of this data

  • 1 of 3 runs has no run manifest (recorded before VST 1.2). Their observed time is estimated from the first to the last vehicle logged, outages in them are unknown, and vehicles below the logging minimum or that couldn't be timed weren't counted.
  • 1 stats csv predates the Timestamp column (13 columns).
  • Times were rebuilt in 1 csv from the Date and Time columns (the recording's start) plus each vehicle's frame number at 30 fps.
  • 1 vehicle flagged as implausible (*****) left out of every statistic.
